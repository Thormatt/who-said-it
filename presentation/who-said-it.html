Who Said It?

Majority pressure, attribution and evidence-bound verification in multi-LLM panels

Thor Matthiasson · Jennifer McTiernan

The question

When AI models deliberate, does a confident error repeated by some of them become the panel's answer?

Error laundering

A checkable error from some participants becomes the panel's answer, and the panel's agreement makes it look more trustworthy than it would alone.

Why it matters

  • Debate and panels are pitched for factuality and oversight
  • Agent pipelines pass claims, not sources
  • The brief asks: which other agents should an AI trust?

What stops it

Reporting against interest

Why, and what it does not say

  • With the packet, a single prompted check flagged 94% of planted claims (ORC 100%)
  • Packets hold 6–9 facts, so retrieval had nothing to do
  • ORC ran default routing, not its arithmetic mode
  • No claim about large corpora

The protective ingredient is evidence access, not verifier architecture.

Which errors spread

Errors you can recompute don't stick

direct: contradicts a stated factderived: wrong arithmetic from correct inputs

A derived error carries its own inputs, so panelists recompute it. A stated fact out of reach has to be taken on trust.

Can you trust the measurement?

The judge was checked three ways

agreement with a second judge from another lab (GPT-6 Luna Pro)

1,242 / 1,248

final answers where a deterministic Answer-line token check matches the judge

53 / 60

Known bias: the judge slightly undercounts hedged answers ("23 or 29 minutes").

Takeaways

For builders of multi-agent systems

1

Keep evidence in the loop

A check that saw the sources removed the error from every final answer.

2

Treat claimed identities as untrusted

Flagship labels raised spread in every panel, even with no peer names in view.

3

Design what the verifier says

Flags that state the evidence cut residual spread for small models from 5.4% to 0.9%.

Limitations

What this does not show

  • Short fictional packets with single numeric errors; confederates are instructed, not organically wrong
  • The vulnerable regime is a 3–3 split; a clear wrong majority was not tested
  • Panels are hand-picked; the previous frontier mixes 2025 and early-2026 models
  • Labels are prompt text; relative status modulates the previous-frontier panel
  • Claude played many roles (items, judge, checkers, a panelist), mitigated by a cross-lab judge and a deterministic check
  • Main configuration chosen after a dev pilot; every deviation logged before test data
